## 2. Business Nature Analysis (段永平 Framework)

**One‑Sentence Definition**  
Al Rajhi Bank is the largest Islamic bank in Saudi Arabia, providing Sharia‑compliant retail, corporate, and wealth management banking services, with a dominant deposit franchise and a low‑cost operating model.

**Is this “the right business”?**  
- **Yes**, because it possesses several characteristics of a high‑quality compounder:  
  - **Recurring revenue:** Deposits and fee‑based services generate stable income.  
  - **High margins:** Cost‑to‑income of 27% is among the best in global banking.  
  - **Low capital intensity** compared to manufacturing – minimal CapEx, high ROE.  
  - **Strong operating leverage:** Revenue growth flows disproportionately to net income (fixed cost base).  

**Business Model**  
- **Retail banking (~45% of revenue):** Mortgage lending (government‑subsidised via Sakani), personal loans, credit cards.  
- **Corporate banking (~30%):** Working capital, trade finance, project finance (infrastructure).  
- **Investments & Treasury (~20%):** Sukuk, equity holdings, interbank.  
- **Wealth management & fees (~5%).**  

**Margins & Operating Leverage**  
- Net interest margin (NIM) expanded in 2023–2024 due to high rates but may compress as Saudi rates follow Fed cuts.  
- The bank’s low cost structure gives it a buffer: every 1% revenue change translates to ~1.5% net income change (high operating leverage).  
- Fee income is growing (digital banking, asset management) and is less interest‑rate sensitive.

**Duan’s “Right Business” Score: 4.5/5**  
- Only demerit: banking is inherently levered to economic cycles and regulation; no business is truly permanent.

**What Could Destroy the Moat in 10 Years?**

## 4. Inverse Thinking & Risk (Munger Framework)

**“How could Al Rajhi Bank fail?”**

1. **Asset Quality Shock**  
   - Saudi real estate is over‑leveraged? Al Rajhi’s mortgage book (~35% of loans) is largely government‑backed (subsidised payments), so risk is lower, but a drop in oil prices or employment could cause defaults.  
   - Historical analogy: **Spanish cajas (2008)** – excessive real estate lending led to collapse. Al Rajhi is far more conservative, but the risk exists.

2. **Interest Rate Risk / Margin Compression**  
   - If Saudi rates fall faster than expected, NIM could compress to 3.0% or below. EPS could drop 15–20%.  
   - **Anchoring:** We may assume margins stay high; they will normalise.

3. **Competition from Digital Banks**  
   - Saudi Arabia is promoting fintech (e.g., digital bank “Saudi Digital Bank”, STC Pay). Younger customers may switch for better UX.  
   - Munger’s lesson: “If you have a strong moat, you can survive, but never underestimate the pace of competitive imitation.”

4. **Management / Succession Risk**  
   - Current CEO Waleed Al‑Mogbel is highly regarded, but the board is heavily influenced by the founding family. If a less capable family member takes over, capital allocation quality may decline.  
   - Historical analogy: **Wells Fargo’s cross‑selling scandal** – a once‑great culture deteriorated.

5. **Geopolitical / Regulatory Risk**  
   - Saudi Arabia faces regional tensions (Yemen, Iran). A major escalation could hurt confidence and asset values.  
   - Also, government may increase “taxes” on banks (e.g., higher capital requirements, levy on profits).

6. **Valuation Risk**  
   - Currently at 12.9× P/E – not expensive, but if earnings contract 15% and multiple stays same, price could fall to ~55 SAR.  
   - Bear case: P/E compresses to 11× (past crisis level), price = 56 SAR.

**Cross‑Disciplinary Check**  
- **Biology:** Large organisms (banks) can be slow to adapt. Al Rajhi’s size is a double‑edged sword.  
- **Physics:** Mean reversion of ROE – as the bank grows, ROE tends to decline (law of large numbers).  
- **Psychology:** Confirmation bias – we see low P/E and assume undervaluation, but maybe the market is discounting a structural decline.

**Narrative Bias Check**  
- The “Vision 2030” story is real but already priced in. The stock’s underperformance since April suggests the narrative may be fading.

## 6. Industry & Civilization Trends (李录 Framework)

**Secular Trend or Cyclical Play?**  
- Saudi banking is **secular** due to:  
  - **Demographics:** Young population (65% under 35) entering the banking system.  
  - **Vision 2030:** Massive infrastructure spending, higher homeownership, SME lending.  
  - **Financial deepening:** Saudi’s credit‑to‑GDP ratio is low (~60%) vs. developed markets (100%+), suggesting long growth runway.  
- However, **interest rates are cyclical**. The current high‑rate environment will normalise, impacting margins.

**S‑Curve Position**  
- The industry is in the **middle of the S‑curve** – steady growth, not explosive.  
- Al Rajhi is a mature leader; its growth will track GDP growth (~3‑4% real) plus inflation (~2%) = ~5‑6% revenue growth long‑term.

**Li Lu’s Question: “Is this a civilizational paradigm shift?”**  
- No – banking is not being disrupted; it’s evolving. But the shift to digital and Islamic finance is a regional paradigm. Al Rajhi is well positioned.  
- **10‑20 Year Thesis:** Saudi Arabia will become a bigger financial hub; Al Rajhi will be a prime beneficiary. Tailwinds from privatisation, foreign inflows, and capital market development.

**Risks to Industry Thesis**  
- Oil price collapse could slow Vision 2030.  
- Geopolitical instability.  
- Over‑regulation (e.g., forced reduction in bank fees).
