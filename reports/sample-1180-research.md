## Step 2: Business Nature Analysis (Duan Yongping)

**One-line definition:** Saudi National Bank is the largest Sharia-compliant commercial bank in Saudi Arabia, serving retail, corporate, and investment clients with a dominant market share (~25% of banking assets).

**Is this the “right business”?**  
Yes — a leading bank in a regulatory-protected, high-margin, capital-intensive industry. SNB benefits from:
- **Scalable infrastructure:** Physical branch network + digital channels already costed.
- **Recurring revenue:** Net interest income (70% of revenue) and fee-based income.
- **Operating leverage:** As the economy grows (Vision 2030), incremental revenue drops largely to profit.

**Margins structure:**  
- Net interest margin (NIM): 3.4% (healthy, above 3%).  
- Cost-to-income ratio: ~36% (efficient for a large bank).  
- ROE: 18% (consistently above cost of equity).  

**Operating leverage:** Low fixed-asset intensity; cost growth lags revenue growth during expansion. The bank’s digital transformation will further reduce marginal costs.

**Concern:** Like all banks, SNB is leveraged to GDP and interest rates. A slowing Saudi economy or falling oil prices would compress NIM and raise provisions. The “right business” label depends on the macro tailwind remaining intact.

| Moat Type | Score (1–5) | Evidence & Commentary |

| **Scale** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Largest bank by assets in KSA: ~$250bn. Branch network >500, ATMs >2,000. Scale provides cost advantage in funding (low-cost deposits) and distribution. Competitors cannot replicate quickly. |

| **Network Effects** | ⭐⭐ | Some network effect in payments (large merchant base attracts users), but limited relative to tech platforms. |
| **Tech / Patents** | ⭐⭐ | Digital banking is good but not patent-protected. Open banking regulation will increase competition. |

**Moat Trend:** **Narrowing slowly**. The core scale and switching cost moats are durable, but technology-driven disruption (fintech, open banking) is eroding the advantages. Over 10 years, SNB’s moat will be shallower unless it becomes a leading digital bank itself.

**What could destroy the moat in 10 years?**  
- A fully digital competitor (e.g., Alinma Bank or a foreign fintech) capturing 15%+ market share.  
- Regulatory changes allowing non-bank lenders to take deposits.  
- A major cyberattack that destroys trust.  
- The PIF forcing consolidation that dilutes SNB’s standalone advantages.

## Step 4: Inverse Thinking & Risk (Munger)

**How could this investment lose 50% or more?**

1. **Oil price collapse** → Saudi government spending cut → recession → loan defaults + NIM compression. Historical analogy: 2015–2016 oil crash, Saudi banks saw 30–40% drawdowns. SNB’s exposure to government-linked projects makes it vulnerable.

2. **Real estate bubble burst** → SNB is heavily exposed to mortgages (50%+ of loan book). A 30% correction in residential real estate would cripple asset quality. Region: UAE 2008 experience.

3. **Fintech disruption accelerated** → If regulators allow STC Pay to offer full banking services without the overhead, SNB could lose low-cost deposit franchise. Analogy: Indian PSU banks vs. HDFC Bank.

4. **Management overconfidence (post-merger hubris)** → SNB’s 2021 merger was executed well, but the “best team” may pursue expensive international acquisitions or over-leverage.

5. **Political / regulatory risk** → Sudden tax on banks, forced lending to Vision 2030 projects with low returns, or nationalization of profits.

6. **Currency peg collapse** (low probability but devastating) → SAR is pegged to USD. If Saudi de-pegs due to fiscal strain, bank balance sheets would be severely disrupted.

**Narrative bias check:** The “Vision 2030” story is widely accepted. But the actual economic diversification results have been modest. Oil still funds 70%+ of government revenue. If Vision 2030 fails to attract enough FDI, bank loan growth may disappoint.

**Survivorship bias:** We study SNB because it survived and thrived. But many large Middle East banks (e.g., Lebanon’s banks) collapsed under political pressure. Saudi stability is not absolute.

**Verdict:** B+ management. Professional, capital-allocation neutral. They won’t destroy value, but they may not create exceptional returns either.

## Step 6: Industry & Civilization Trends (Li Lu)

**Is Saudi banking at a civilizational inflection point?**  
No — it’s a **mature cyclical** industry, not a paradigm shift. Banking is a slow-moving, regulated business. However, three secular trends matter:

1. **Saudi GDP growth (Vision 2030):** Government spending on infrastructure, tourism, and giga-projects will drive loan demand for 10+ years. This is a positive tailwind, but not exponential.

2. **Digital banking / open banking:** This is a civilization-level shift in consumer behavior. SNB is investing heavily, but faces threat from agile fintechs. The outcome is uncertain.

3. **Islamic finance expansion:** SNB is a leader in Sharia-compliant products. The global Islamic finance market is growing ~10% CAGR, which could provide incremental fee income.

**S-curve position:**  
- Core banking (deposits, loans) is at the **top of S-curve** — mature, low growth.  
- Digital banking is in **early acceleration** — SNB must ride this curve or be disrupted.  
- Wealth management / investment banking is in **mid-curve** — potential for above-GDP growth.

**Li Lu’s framework:** This is a **cyclical compounder** — good business in a growing economy, but not a civilization-shaping holding. A 10–20 year secular trend requires the Vision 2030 transformation to sustain 4–5% real GDP growth. If it fails, SNB reverts to a slow-growth utility.

## Step 7: Valuation & Margin of Safety

### Approach 1: Dividend Discount Model (Gordon Growth)

- Current dividend: 1.35 SAR/share (FY2024) → yield 3.46% at 39 SAR.  
- Growth rate: 5% (in-line with GDP + mild inflation)  
- Cost of equity: 10% (CAPM: risk-free 4% + beta 0.9 × equity risk premium 6.5%)  

**Fair value = 1.35 × 1.05 / (0.10 - 0.05) = 28.35 SAR**  
This indicates overvaluation. But the model is highly sensitive to growth assumptions. If growth is 6% → fair value = 35.8 SAR; if 7% → 50.6 SAR.  

### Approach 2: Historical P/E and P/B

| Metric | Historical Average | Current | Implied Fair Price |
|--------|-------------------|---------|---------------------|
| P/E (5-yr avg) | 12.5x | 7.1x | 68.5 SAR (if earnings maintained) |
| P/B (5-yr avg) | 1.6x | 1.27x | 49.0 SAR (equity 115bn, shares 3.75bn) |

**Fair price range: 49–68 SAR** based on multiples alone. The current price (39) implies a deep discount to history.

### Approach 3: Reverse DCF (current price → implied growth)

At 39 SAR, with COE 10% and dividend 1.35, the implied perpetual growth rate is:  
39 = 1.35 × (1+g) / (0.10 - g) → g ≈ 6.3%  
This is achievable if Saudi GDP grows 4–5% and SNB captures slightly higher fee income. So the market is pricing in a mid-range growth — not overly optimistic.

### Scenario Analysis

| Scenario | Terminal P/E | Fair Value | Upside/(Downside) |
|----------|-------------|------------|-------------------|
| **Bull** (strong Vision 2030, 8% earnings growth, P/E 15x) | 15x | 67 SAR | +72% |
| **Base** (5% growth, P/E 12x) | 12x | 54 SAR | +38% |
| **Bear** (recession, growth 2%, P/E 9x) | 9x | 31 SAR | -21% |

**Margin of Safety at 39 SAR:**  
- Base case fair value ~54 SAR → 28% margin of safety.  
- Bear case floor ~31 SAR → downside of 21%.  
- Probability-weighted: expected value = 0.25×67 + 0.5×54 + 0.25×31 = 51.5 SAR → 32% upside.

**Compelling entry:** A price below 35 SAR (P/E ~6.5x) would provide 40%+ margin of safety to base case. At 39 SAR, the risk/reward is moderately attractive.
